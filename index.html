SQS² Saudi Quantum Software Stack

A national, hardware-agnostic stack that takes quantum programs from Python, C++ or Rust down to the pulses that drive the qubits, and runs them alongside high-performance computing.

Contact the project lead How the stack works

Overview

Quantum computers will work beside supercomputers as accelerators. Using them takes software that compiles one program for different quantum hardware, runs it reliably and coordinates it with HPC.

SQS² builds that layer for Saudi Arabia: vendor-neutral, based on open standards, and a stack national institutions can operate, extend and trust.

Why it matters

A sovereign stack lets the Kingdom adopt quantum computing on its own terms and builds national expertise in quantum software, in support of Vision 2030 goals in energy, materials, finance and logistics.

How it works

Five layers lower a program from the user’s code to the pulses that drive the qubits. Figure 1 follows one program through them.

  1. L1 Applications & SDKsPython, C++ and Rust interfaces, with support for common quantum frameworks.
  2. L2 Quantum programming interfaceStandard APIs for submitting jobs and discovering available devices.
  3. L3 AI-powered toolchainCompilation and circuit optimisation that adapt to the current state of the hardware.
  4. L4 Platform managerOne abstraction over all quantum processors, with scheduling across them.
  5. L5 Quantum control planeReal-time control and measurement of one or more quantum processors.
  6. QPU Quantum processorsSuperconducting and neutral-atom devices, driven by the pulses that L5 generates.

Research directions

Hardware-agnostic programming

Open intermediate representations, OpenQASM 3 and QIR, let one program target devices from different vendors.

HPC integration

Hybrid quantum–classical jobs run through the schedulers HPC centres already use, such as Slurm.

AI across the stack

Machine learning for compilation, calibration, readout and error mitigation.

Distributed quantum control

Coordinating several connected quantum processors as one resource.

Hardware platforms

SQS² is developed and tested on laboratory hardware, from cryogenic systems to control electronics, and targets two qubit technologies.

Superconducting qubits

Microwave circuits on a chip, cooled to about 10 mK. Each gate is a shaped pulse tens of nanoseconds long, so the lowest layer of the stack works in pulses, not gates.

Neutral atoms

Single atoms held in optical tweezers. Loading is random, so atoms are rearranged into a defect-free register before each run.

Cryogenics and control

A dilution refrigerator cools the processor in stages from 50 K to 10 mK. Controllers and FPGA boards generate and read the pulses in real time.
